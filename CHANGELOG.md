# Changelog

What changed in each release of the NE-CAT beamline guides, newest first. Every page shows its release number and the date it was last updated at its foot.

## 1.7 — 2026-11-13

- New five-minute guide, "Why are we freezing our crystals?": what X-rays do to a crystal, why keeping it at 173 degrees below zero makes it last, how a crystal that is half water is cooled without turning to ice, and how cold that is. Four stops, no equations.
- The index lists it in the experiment series.

## 1.6.1 — 2026-11-13

- "A walk down the beamline": the beamstop is now drawn where it is on the beamline, just behind the crystal, not on the detector. The text and the caption say so.
- "How does an X-ray detector work?": the grey disc in the middle of the detector in the opening figure is removed.

## 1.6 — 2026-11-13

- New five-minute guide, "How does an X-ray detector work?": 18 million pixels that each count X-rays one at a time, with no pause between images, at a distance chosen for each crystal. Four stops, no equations. Its opening figure is a simulated data collection, and its last figure shows which of the rays from the crystal the detector catches as it is moved.
- The detector on 24-ID-C, an EIGER2 XE 16M, is drawn as a cartoon of the real one in this guide and at the last stop of "A walk down the beamline".
- The index lists the new guide in the beamline series.

## 1.5.2 — 2026-11-13

- "How do we see a protein?": the helices of myoglobin are now colored from the HELIX records of entry 1MBN. One helix ends one amino acid earlier than before.

## 1.5.1 — 2026-11-13

- Corrections from a review of the open questions in the live guides.
- "How APS works": the beam lifetime for the two fill patterns in use now starts from 3 hours, the middle of the 2 to 4 hours reported in October 2025, in place of earlier guesses. The length of the accumulator ring, 30.7 m, is now taken from the APS parameters page.
- The detector on 24-ID-C is named as an EIGER2 XE 16M, with about 18 million pixels, in "How NE-CAT works" and "A walk down the beamline".
- "How do we see a protein?": one word changed in the note on beamline software.
- The build sources in `src/` are no longer served on the web site. They remain in the repository.

## 1.5 — 2026-11-13

- New five-minute guide, "How do we see a protein?": why light cannot show atoms, what the crystal is for, how the spots become a map, and how atoms are fitted into it. Four stops, no equations. It opens the series on the experiment.
- Its figures use a real protein, sperm whale myoglobin, drawn from entry 1MBN in the Protein Data Bank. One figure zooms from a hair down to two atoms of the protein, and two others pull back from the atoms of one helix to the whole molecule.
- The index lists it in the experiment series.

## 1.4 — 2026-11-13

- New five-minute guide, "A walk down the beamline": five stops from the magnets that make the X-rays to the detector, with no equations. It is the short companion to "How NE-CAT works", and each links to the other.
- The index lists it in the beamline series.

## 1.3 — 2026-11-13

- New guide, "What is a synchrotron?": the first five-minute guide. How electrons are brought to almost the speed of light, held in a ring, and made to give off X-rays, and what the APS Upgrade changed. Five stops, no equations. It is the short companion to "How APS works", and each links to the other.
- Guides now come at two levels, marked by color: yellow for the five-minute guides, black for the in-depth ones. The four earlier guides are in-depth.
- The landing page is now an index. It groups the guides in four series: the machine, the beamline, the experiment, and the science. Buttons at the top show everything, only the five-minute guides, or only the in-depth guides. Adding `?level=intro` or `?level=depth` to the address opens the page on one kind.
- Guides have new addresses with no numbers in them, such as `how-necat-works.html` and `fluorescence.html`, so that adding a guide no longer changes the address of another. Every earlier address redirects to the new one.

## 1.2.1 — 2026-11-11

- Links to the guides now show a card, with a title, a description and a picture, when they are shared in Messages, LinkedIn, Slack and similar apps.
- The site has an icon for browser tabs and home screens.
- No change to the content of the guides.

## 1.2 — 2026-11-11

- Guide 4 is reworked and retitled "X-ray fluorescence at NE-CAT". It now begins with what fluorescence is, which atoms in a protein crystal give it, the edges and lines within reach at 24-ID, the edge scan, and how to identify a metal from a spectrum. The earlier material on scatter and where to put the detector follows, shortened.
- Guide 4 has a new address, `NE-CAT_4_X-ray_Fluorescence.html`. Its earlier addresses redirect to it.
- Every page now shows when it was last updated and links to this file.

## 1.1 — 2026-11-11

- New guide 1, "How APS works": the electron gun and linac, the accumulator ring and booster, the storage-ring magnets and vacuum, the seven-bend lattice, RF and bunches, fill patterns, and swap-out injection.
- The existing guides are renumbered 2 to 4. Their earlier addresses redirect to the new ones.
- The NIH acknowledgment (P30 GM124165) is added to every page.

## 1.0 — 2026-11-11

- First release: a landing page and three guides, "How NE-CAT works", "Why the X-ray beam is polarized" and "Where to put the fluorescence detector".
